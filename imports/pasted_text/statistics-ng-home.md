Mission section

Our Mission

The mission of Statistics NG is to improve decision-making in Nigeria by making data more useful, more predictive, and more actionable.

A lot of organizations already collect data, but most of that data is underused. It sits in spreadsheets, survey files, PDFs, ministry records, field reports, and disconnected databases. Statistics NG takes that scattered information and turns it into a system that people can trust and use.

Our goal is simple:
to help institutions make faster, smarter, and more evidence-based decisions.

The problem we solve

The Problem We Solve

Nigeria has a huge amount of data potential, but several problems keep organizations from benefiting from it.

Data is fragmented
Information is spread across agencies, programs, surveys, field teams, and legacy systems.

Data quality is inconsistent
There are missing values, duplicate entries, weak definitions, outdated records, and poor standardization.

Most analysis is backward-looking
Many organizations can describe what happened, but they cannot predict what is coming or decide what to do next.

Decision-makers are overloaded
Senior leaders do not need long technical reports. They need clear answers, risk signals, forecasts, and priority areas.

Technical insight rarely becomes operational action
Even when the analysis is good, there is often no workflow to turn insight into budget shifts, field action, targeting, or monitoring.

Statistics NG exists to close that gap.

How we work

How Statistics NG Works

Statistics NG operates across the full data value chain.

1. Data Collection and Integration
We pull together data from surveys, administrative records, partner program data, market and price data, facility and service delivery data, satellite and geospatial data, field agent submissions, mobile and digital platform data, and internal client databases. We then structure that data so it can be used consistently and reliably.

2. Data Cleaning and Quality Assurance
Before any model is built, we improve data quality by removing duplicates, correcting formatting issues, handling missing values, validating records, standardizing categories, reconciling conflicting sources, and documenting assumptions and limitations. Weak data produces weak decisions, so this step is essential.

3. Statistical Analysis
We apply strong statistical methods to understand patterns, trends, and relationships in the data. This includes descriptive analysis, trend analysis, regression models, segmentation, panel and longitudinal analysis, causal inference where appropriate, monitoring and evaluation metrics, and experimental or quasi-experimental analysis.

4. Machine Learning and AI
Where useful, we apply machine learning and AI to go beyond description and move into prediction and optimization. This includes forecasting prices, demand, or service pressure, identifying high-risk communities, detecting anomalies or fraud patterns, classifying regions by vulnerability, predicting dropout, stockouts, outbreaks, or yield decline, and using NLP, computer vision, or geospatial AI where relevant.

We do not use AI for hype. We use it where it improves speed, precision, and scale.

5. Decision Products
We turn technical outputs into usable products such as live dashboards, state and LGA scorecards, early warning systems, forecasting tools, priority maps, intervention targeting tools, policy simulation tools, monthly intelligence reports, APIs, data feeds, and executive briefings.

The point is not just to show numbers. The point is to guide action.

6. Monitoring and Impact Tracking
We help clients measure whether their interventions are working by tracking baseline conditions, target populations, implementation progress, outcomes over time, geographic differences, cost-effectiveness, and measurable impact. This makes it easier to improve programs instead of repeating weak approaches.
